# LinkedIn

**Last checked:** November 2026. Tags: [platform] statement or code, [study] third-party data with a sample, [reported] single or vendor source.

## Who's there and what they want
Professionals, buyers, recruiters, and peers. They're scrolling between meetings and reward posts that teach them something or show them how someone actually did the work. The most composed register of any feed: candid and specific, not corporate.

## What the feed rewards right now
- **Perspective over polish.** [platform] LinkedIn added a "seems like AI slop" report option and classifiers (July 2026) that limit out-of-network reach for posts that are "polished but lack an identifiable perspective or substantive value." AI help is allowed; generic is not. [platform] LinkedIn reported classified slop gets about 40% fewer views. LinkedIn also asks members to disclose substantial AI involvement when it isn't apparent.
- **Reading time.** [platform] Dwell time has been a ranking signal since LinkedIn's 2020 engineering write-up; clicking "see more" and reading on counts.
- **Concrete detail.** [study] In a 687,000-post analysis (MagicPost, October 2026), posts without concrete detail ran about 23% below each creator's baseline.
- **Conversation, not reactions.** [reported] Substantive comments count for more than likes, and early replies from the author help.
- **Posting time barely matters.** [study] Same study: posting hour moved final reach by about 2%, and posts keep collecting views for days. Don't optimize the clock.

## What costs reach
- **Links.** [study] A link card cost about 19%, a URL in the text about 21%, and "link in the first comment" about 16%. The comment trick no longer avoids the penalty. If clicks are the goal, accept the cost and say what's behind the link; if reach is the goal, leave the link out or post it later.
- **Keyword-comment bait.** [study] "Comment GUIDE and I'll DM you" closers went from a small lift to about 47% below baseline after a July 2026 change. Other comment requests lost about 19%. A plain closing question was unaffected.
- **Polls** underperformed photos by about 22% [study]; they drive votes, not authority.
- **Hashtag walls.** Zero to three, at the end, only if the author uses them.

## Formats

### Text post (the default)
- **Length:** 150 to 300 words (roughly 900 to 1,800 characters). Max 3,000 characters.
- **The fold:** about 210 characters (two to three lines on mobile) show before "see more." That's the whole pitch: the claim, the number, or the moment, with no warm-up.
- **Shape:** short paragraphs of one to three sentences, one idea each. White space between paragraphs, not between every sentence (that's broetry).
- **Structures that work:** story to lesson (what happened, what it cost, what changed); a specific result and how; a contrarian take with receipts; a short how-to with numbered steps; a teardown of something real.
- **Closer:** the takeaway in one line, or a specific question ("What did your team use before you moved off Jenkins?"), never "Thoughts?"
- **Tagging:** only people who are actually in the story, and only when they'd be glad to be tagged.

### Document post (carousel)
- 5 to 10 slides, one idea per slide, large type.
- Slide 1 makes a promise the deck keeps; the last slide lands the takeaway or a single next step.
- Its lead over photo posts has shrunk to about 9% [study], so use it when the content is genuinely sequential (steps, a framework, a before-and-after), not by default.
- The caption still needs a real hook; it's what shows in the feed.

### Comment (on someone else's post)
Add something: a related experience, a number, a respectful disagreement, a question that moves the thread. "Great post!" is invisible.

## Checklist
- [ ] The first two lines would make the target reader stop, on their own
- [ ] The author's stake is in the post: a number, an event, a mistake, an opinion
- [ ] At least one concrete detail per claim
- [ ] No engagement bait, no keyword-DM closer, no broetry
- [ ] Link decision made on purpose (or no link)
- [ ] Under 3,000 characters; hook inside the first ~210

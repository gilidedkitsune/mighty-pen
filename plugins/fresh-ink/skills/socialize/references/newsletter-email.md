# Newsletter and email

**Last checked:** November 2026. Tags: [platform] statement, [study] third-party data with a sample, [reported] single or vendor source.

## Newsletter section (promoting a piece inside a newsletter)
- 100 to 250 words, in the newsletter's own voice: a note from a person to their readers, not a press release.
- Open with why it matters to this list now, then the one or two most useful atoms, then the link with a plain description of what's behind it.
- One link per section where possible.

## Promotional or nurture email
- **Subject line:** 30 to 50 characters. Run headline-lab for options when it matters, and offer an A/B pair from two different angles.
- **Preview text:** adds to the subject, never repeats it.
- **The first sentence carries the pitch.** [platform] Gmail's AI Inbox (rolling out to paid Workspace accounts from October 2026) summarizes the opening lines, so a slow warm-up can disappear from the summary entirely. Judge subject-line tests on clicks and replies, not opens.
- **Body:** 75 to 200 words, one atom, one call to action, matched to the reader's stage (awareness: read or watch; consideration: compare or see proof; decision: start, book, buy).
- **Avoid:** all-caps urgency, fake "quick question" or "re:" subjects, emoji overload, and any claim the landing page doesn't back up.

## Checklist
- [ ] Subject 30 to 50 characters; preview text adds something new
- [ ] The pitch is in the first sentence
- [ ] One call to action
- [ ] Every stat carries its source
